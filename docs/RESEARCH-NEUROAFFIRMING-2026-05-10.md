# Neurodiversity-Affirming Rubric Update — Research Findings (2026-05-10)

Scope: workplace empathy (responder), school self-advocacy (initiator), interview prep (responder). Goal: integrate post-2018 peer-reviewed and ASAN-grade evidence so ScenarioLab does not penalize valid neurodivergent communication patterns. All citations are real and verifiable; claims without a real source were dropped.

## Executive summary
- **The double empathy problem (Milton 2012; Crompton et al. 2020a, 2020b; Heasman & Gillespie 2019) reframes "social deficit" as a bidirectional mismatch.** Autistic-to-autistic information transfer is as efficient as NT-to-NT, and rapport drops only in *mixed* dyads. ScenarioLab must therefore score communicative *content*, not whether the user matches a neurotypical interaction style.
- **First impressions of autistic adults are penalized within seconds based on *style*, not *substance* (Sasson et al. 2017; Whelpley & May 2023).** When the same content is delivered by transcript, the bias disappears or reverses. This is direct empirical justification for our rubrics never scoring tone, warmth, vocal variety, eye contact, or charisma.
- **For ADHD, emotional dysregulation and rejection sensitivity (Beheshti et al. 2020 systematic review; Bisby et al. 2024 qualitative) are core, not peripheral.** Feedback delivery to the user must therefore be specific, actionable, and asset-based, never globally evaluative ("you came across as cold/rude/scattered").

## Section 1 — Autism communication science

- **Double empathy (Milton 2012, *Disability & Society* 27(6); doi:10.1080/09687599.2012.710008).** Communicative breakdown between autistic and non-autistic people is bidirectional, rooted in differing dispositions and shared-experience gaps — not a one-way autistic deficit. **Means for rubrics:** Never score "the partner found you hard to read" as a deduction; score whether the user delivered the *content* (acknowledgment, ask, STAR element).
- **Autistic-to-autistic information transfer is as efficient as NT-to-NT (Crompton, Ropar, Evans-Williams, Flynn & Fletcher-Watson 2020, *Autism* 24(7):1704-1712; PMID 32431157; doi:10.1177/1362361320919286).** In an 8-person diffusion chain, detail retention dropped only in *mixed* chains. **Means for rubrics:** Whatever is "off" in NT-autistic interaction is interactional, not in the autistic speaker. Don't penalize unfamiliar register or different turn-taking rhythm.
- **Neurotype matching, not autism, drives rapport (Crompton, Hallett, Ropar, Flynn & Fletcher-Watson 2020, *Frontiers in Psychology* 11:586171; PMID 33192918; doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2020.586171).** Both autistic and non-autistic raters report higher rapport when the partner shares their neurotype. **Means for rubrics:** "Felt rapport" is not a property of the speaker; do not let evaluator-LLM proxies for rapport (warmth, charm) influence scoring.
- **Distinctive autistic interaction features include "generous assumption of common ground" and "low demand for coordination" (Heasman & Gillespie 2019, *Autism* 23(4):910-921; PMID 30073872; doi:10.1177/1362361318785172).** Conversations among autistic adults run on different but functional intersubjective norms. **Means for rubrics:** Treat brevity, low-coordination openings, and skipping social warm-up as neutral, not negative.
- **Monotropism is the dominant autistic-led theory of autism (Murray, Lesser & Lawson 2005, *Autism* 9(2):139-156; PMID 15857859; doi:10.1177/1362361305051398; validated by Garau et al. 2023, Monotropism Questionnaire pre-print).** Attention is concentrated rather than distributed, producing hyperfocused depth on topics of interest. **Means for rubrics:** Long, deep, on-topic answers (info-dump that is *responsive*) are a strength, not verbosity to deduct.
- **Bias against autistic people fires within thin slices and is style-driven, not content-driven (Sasson et al. 2017, *Scientific Reports* 7:40700; PMID 28145411; doi:10.1038/srep40700).** When evaluators see only transcripts (no audio/video), the negative bias disappears. **Means for rubrics:** A text/transcript-graded rubric is the more equitable surface; never re-introduce style-as-content via "warmth" criteria.
- **Disclosure + neurodiversity training together close most of the autism interview hiring gap (Whelpley, May, Schoenherr et al. 2025, *J. Autism Dev. Disord.*; PMID 39888555 / PMID 39964656; doi:10.1007/s10803-024-06713-8 and 10.1007/s10803-025-06751-w).** Training alone or disclosure alone does not. **Means for rubrics:** Disclosure is one valid path the user may choose — score the *self-advocacy ask*, not the disclosure decision itself.
- **Empathy expression in autistic adults is often present and practical, not absent (Fletcher-Watson & Bird 2020, *Autism* 24(1):3-6; doi:10.1177/1362361319883506; alexithymia hypothesis from Bird & Cook 2013, *Translational Psychiatry* 3:e285; doi:10.1038/tp.2013.61).** Reduced *expressive* empathy markers often track alexithymia (a co-occurring trait) rather than autism per se; affective concern is intact. **Means for rubrics:** Score empathic *content* (acknowledgment, reflection, autonomy-respecting offer), not warmth performance.
- **Camouflaging/masking has measurable mental health costs (Hull et al. 2017, "Putting on My Best Normal", *J. Autism Dev. Disord.* 47(8):2519-2534; PMID 28527095; doi:10.1007/s10803-017-3166-5; Hull et al. 2020 CAT-Q gender study, doi:10.1177/1362361319864804; Beck et al. 2020, *Autism* 24(4):809-821; PMID 32429817).** Camouflaging is associated with exhaustion, anxiety, depression, and suicidality. **Means for rubrics:** Avoid coaching that *requires* the user to perform NT mannerisms. Frame any "convention-aware" tips as optional information about the listener, not a behavioral demand.

## Section 2 — ADHD adult communication science

- **Emotional dysregulation is a core, not peripheral, feature of adult ADHD (Beheshti, Chavanon & Christiansen 2020, *PLOS One*; doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0280131; Wehmeier, Schacht & Barkley 2010, *J. Adolescent Health* 46(3):209-217; PMID 20159496; doi:10.1016/j.jadohealth.2009.09.009).** Impairments in close relationships, work, and academics correlate more strongly with dysregulation than with inattention/hyperactivity alone. **Means for rubrics:** Feedback that produces shame spirals undermines the user's ability to act on it. Frame next steps concretely, not globally.
- **Rejection-Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD) is real as lived experience even if not a DSM construct (Bisby et al. 2024 medRxiv preprint of qualitative ADHD rejection-sensitivity study; doi:10.1101/2024.11.16.24317418; subsequent peer-reviewed publication PMID 41564005). Coined clinically by Dodson (popular literature, ADDitude).** Adults describe perceived rejection as somatic ("punched in the chest"), with rumination and self-blame. **Means for rubrics:** The feedback delivery layer (the LLM next-step) must avoid global trait language ("you sounded rude"), use behavior-specific descriptions, and pair every gap with a concrete next attempt.
- **Time blindness / time-perception deficits are measurable in adult ADHD (Weissenberger et al. 2021, *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 12:680236; PMID PMC8293837; review by Nejati & Yazdani 2023, *Brain Sciences* 13(2):255; PMID 36833791).** Interval reproduction and estimation are impaired. **Means for rubrics:** In interview/STAR prep, do not deduct for absent precise dates; reward concrete *anchors* (year, project, role) without requiring exact timelines.
- **Working memory + response inhibition are central to Barkley's executive-function model of ADHD (Barkley 1997, *Psychological Bulletin* 121(1):65-94; PMID 9000892; doi:10.1037/0033-2909.121.1.65; updated BUEFM, Barkley 2022, *Applied Neuropsychology: Adult* 32(1); doi:10.1080/23279095.2022.2158441).** Holding the question while building a response is genuinely harder. **Means for rubrics:** In behavioral interviews, reward asking for a re-read, taking a beat, restating the prompt, or labeling structure ("Situation: …") — these are valid working-memory supports, not weakness signals.
- **ADHD conversational tangentiality reflects associative-thought variance, especially in unconstrained contexts (Kucyi et al. 2025, *Scientific Reports* 15; doi:10.1038/s41598-025-93053-x; mind-wandering review Bozhilova et al. 2018, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*; PMID PMC6525148).** Tangents often *connect* in associative ways and surface relevant context. **Means for rubrics:** A tangent that ultimately lands on the answer is not a deduction. Penalize only failure to deliver the required content (e.g., no STAR Result), not the path taken to get there.
- **Adults with ADHD show measurably high sensitivity to criticism and benefit from strengths-based reframing (Beaton et al. 2022, "Experiences of criticism in adults with ADHD: A qualitative study", *PLOS One*; PMID PMC8856522; Sedgwick-Müller et al. 2022/Sedgwick et al. 2019 silver-linings thematic analysis, *BMC Psychiatry*; PMC10551976).** Self-reported strength themes: cognitive dynamism, courage, energy, humanity, resilience, transcendence. **Means for rubrics:** The feedback frame should name what worked first, then name a single, specific gap with a concrete next attempt.
- **Antshel & Russo 2019 distinguish autism vs. ADHD social-communication signatures (Antshel & Russo 2019, *Current Psychiatry Reports* 21(5):34; PMID 30903299; doi:10.1007/s11920-019-1020-5).** ADHD social difficulty is often the *presence* of culturally non-normative behaviors (interrupting, intrusion); autism social difficulty is often the *absence* of expected social initiations. **Means for rubrics:** A user interrupting their own answer to clarify is interest-driven engagement, not rudeness. A user not opening with small talk is not a deduction.

## Section 3 — AuDHD overlap

- **Lifetime co-occurrence of ADHD in autistic individuals pools at ~40% (Hours, Recasens & Baleyte 2022, *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 13:837424; doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2022.837424; meta-analysis Lai et al. 2019, *Lancet Psychiatry*; meta-analysis published 2021, doi:10.1016/j.rasd.2021.101750).** AuDHD is the rule, not the exception, in adult clinical populations. **Means for rubrics:** Both rubric profiles (autism patterns + ADHD patterns) can apply to the same user; avoid scoring conventions that one profile favors at the cost of the other.
- **AuDHD compounds executive-function and social-communication challenges (Jaisle, Groves, Black & Aaron 2023, *Research on Child & Adolescent Psychopathology*; PMID PMC9913618; doi:10.1007/s10802-022-00982-6).** Emotion dysregulation mediates the link between combined ADHD+autism symptomatology and social impairment. **Means for rubrics:** Feedback should target the *specific behavior gap*, not infer global social competence.
- **AuDHDers score highest on monotropism (Garau et al. 2023, Monotropism Questionnaire pre-print, OSF; Dwyer et al. 2024, *Neurodiversity*).** **Means for rubrics:** Long, deep, on-topic responses are valid in all three scenarios; the gating criterion is on-topic, not on-length.
- **Camouflaging extends beyond autism to ADHD adults (van der Putten et al. 2024, *Autism Research* 17(4):894-905; doi:10.1002/aur.3099).** **Means for rubrics:** Don't reward "polished" delivery as a proxy for skill — it may be camouflage, with mental health costs.

## Section 4 — Per-scenario implications

### Workplace empathy (rubric: empathy_v1)

The current rubric (Noora + MITI 4.2.1) is well-grounded. Reinforcements and additions below.

**Additions for `do_not_score`:**
- Affective mirroring or "feeling-with" performance — alexithymia hypothesis (Bird & Cook 2013) and Fletcher-Watson & Bird (2020) show practical empathy is valid empathy.
- "Emotional reciprocity" / "shared affect" language — bidirectional rapport is a property of the dyad, not the user (Crompton et al. 2020a/b).
- Length penalty for extended on-topic responses — monotropism (Murray et al. 2005; Garau et al. 2023). A long, deeply attentive response that stays on the coworker's experience is *more* attentive, not less.
- Tangent penalty when the tangent associatively connects back — ADHD associative-thought research (Kucyi et al. 2025; Bozhilova et al. 2018). Score whether the response *lands* on D1-D5, not whether it goes straight there.

**Additions for `evaluator_directive`:**
- "If the response is brief and direct (e.g., 'That's hard. Tired and unfocused is rough.') and hits D1+D3+D4, it scores 6+/10. Do not deduct for missing pleasantries, exclamation points, or affective intensifiers."
- "Practical-support offers ('want me to take the 3pm meeting?') count fully toward D5 — *practical* empathy is empathy (Fletcher-Watson & Bird 2020)."

**Notes on avatar (Alex) personality:**
- Alex shouldn't ramp escalation if the user is brief and on-content. Brevity is not avoidance.
- Alex should not reward extra warmth tokens ("aww", "I'm so sorry") with extra openness signals — that biases users toward NT scripts and risks pushing camouflaging behavior (Hull et al. 2017; Beck et al. 2020).

### School self-advocacy (rubric: self_advocacy_v1)

The current rubric (I PLAN + ASAN Navigating College) is strong. Reinforcements below.

**Additions for `do_not_score`:**
- Apology omission — direct asks without apology padding are valid (ASAN 2013; reinforced by Heasman & Gillespie 2019 finding that autistic interaction tolerates lower coordination overhead).
- Disclosure depth — ADA Title I (42 U.S.C. § 12112) requires only that the user request reasonable accommodation; naming a diagnosis is optional (ADA National Network legal brief on ASD employment; Lindsay, Cagliostro & Carafa 2018, *Disability and Rehabilitation* systematic review; PMID 28797182; doi:10.1080/09638288.2017.1363828). "I have a documented disability and a DRC letter" satisfies D2 fully.
- Hedge-stripped clarity — over-hedging actively *reduces* clarity-of-ask (D1) per I PLAN; absence of hedges is neutral, not bluntness.
- Anxious affect markers in transcript — typing speed, exclamation points, "lol", trailing dots are not D1-D5 signals.

**Additions for `evaluator_directive`:**
- "If the user delivers a direct ask without apology, do not pattern-match to 'rude'. ASAN's *Navigating College* and the I PLAN evidence base (Van Reusen et al. 1994, 2002) both treat directness as the target behavior."
- "If the user names accommodation needs without naming a diagnosis, score D2 the same as a diagnosis-named version. Disclosure decisions carry real costs (Lindsay et al. 2018; PMC10197981 'To tell or not to tell')."

**Notes on avatar (Dr. Patel-style professor):**
- Should not require small-talk warm-up before the ask. Treat a directly-led email as professional, not curt.
- If user discloses, the avatar should respond with "what would help?" rather than asking for diagnostic detail — practitioner-grade norm (AskEARN / Job Accommodation Network guidance).

### Interview prep (rubric: interview_v1)

The current rubric (Campion 1988, Levashina 2014, Maras 2021) is the strongest of the three. Additions below.

**Additions for `do_not_score`:**
- Time-precision penalty — ADHD time-perception research (Weissenberger et al. 2021; Nejati & Yazdani 2023) shows interval estimation is impaired. Reward "in 2024", "last quarter", "on the QA team" without requiring exact dates.
- Pause / "let me think" / re-read request — Barkley working-memory model (Barkley 1997, 2022); already in the rubric, reinforcing.
- STAR-element labeling penalty — explicit labeling ("Situation: …") is a valid working-memory scaffold, not awkwardness. Score it as full D2 = 2.
- Tangent penalty when answer ultimately lands all 4 STAR elements — associative-thought research (Kucyi et al. 2025).
- Disclosure penalty *or* bonus — Whelpley & May (and follow-up Schoenherr et al. 2025) show disclosure only helps with trained interviewers; it should be the user's choice. Do not score it.
- Camouflaging-style polish bonus — van der Putten et al. 2024; Hull et al. 2017. Smooth narrative is not better content.

**Additions for `evaluator_directive`:**
- "Score the four STAR elements regardless of order, prosodic delivery, or whether the candidate explicitly labels them. A bullet list of S/T/A/R is a *full* answer (Maras et al. 2021, *Autism* 25(4):1060-1075; doi:10.1177/1362361320981319; PMID 33339462; PMC8108109)."
- "Reward the clarification turn (D1 = 2) when the user asks for the prompt to be re-read or asks 'do you mean X or Y' — Maras et al. 2021 showed adapted/explicit questions removed most of the autism rating gap."

**Notes on avatar (interviewer):**
- Should rephrase or break a vague prompt into parts when the user asks — this is the experimental adaptation that closed the gap in Maras et al. 2021, not a leniency favor.
- Should not deliver feedback that mentions "confidence", "executive presence", "energy", or "polish". These are NT thin-slice constructs (Sasson et al. 2017).

## Section 5 — Feedback delivery to the user (RSD-aware framing)

The next-step / coaching message that ScenarioLab generates after scoring is where neurodivergent users are most exposed. Apply the following framing standards.

**Favor:**
- Behavior-specific, situated descriptions: *"In your second sentence you went straight to a fix. Adding one sentence acknowledging her tiredness first hits D1."*
- A single concrete next attempt: *"Try: 'That's hard. Tired-and-unfocused is rough.' Then offer."*
- Affirming what worked first, before what to change (Beaton et al. 2022; Sedgwick et al. 2019 strengths-based ADHD framing).
- Plain-English, concrete language; literal phrasings ("you said X, the rubric expected Y on D3").
- Optionality framing: "*If you want a more conventional register, you could add…* — both versions can score 10/10."

**Avoid:**
- Global trait labels: *"you came across as cold / blunt / awkward / scattered / unconfident"* — pure RSD trigger surface (Bisby et al. 2024) and exactly what Sasson et al. 2017 thin-slice bias attacks.
- Warmth or affect coaching: *"try to sound more empathetic"*, *"add some warmth"* — coaches the user toward camouflaging (Hull et al. 2017; Beck et al. 2020) and contradicts our content-only rubric.
- Pile-up of multiple gaps in one message: ADHD working-memory load (Barkley 1997, 2022). One gap, one next attempt.
- Implicit demand to apologize / soften: contradicts ASAN 2013 and I PLAN evidence.
- Inferring intent or character: *"it sounded like you were rushing"*, *"you didn't seem to care"* — content-only scoring forbids this.
- Comparisons to "what most people do" or "neurotypical expectations" — Crompton et al. 2020a/b show that's a dyad property, not a user property.

**Recommended template:**
```
Score: X/10. What worked: [one specific, named behavior tied to a dimension].
One thing to try next: [single, specific, behavior-level rewrite].
Why: [tie to the dimension and the framework, e.g. "D3 wants a complex reflection — naming the impact, not just the state"].
```
